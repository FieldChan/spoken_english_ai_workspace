# Michael's Chinglish Replacement Checklist

This is the 2-minute scan used in Step 8 of the daily protocol: run today's verbatim transcript against this list.

It is deliberately **not** a copy of `handbook/md/chapter10-chinese-english-replacement.md`. That chapter holds all 51 replacements as reference. This file holds only the patterns that have actually shown up in my own speech or logs, with a hit count, so it stays short enough to scan.

Rules:

- A pattern enters this file the first time it appears in one of my transcripts.
- Increment `Hits` every time it appears again.
- At 3 hits, it becomes an error-bank entry (subject to the active cap of 8).
- At 0 hits for a full 6-week cycle, retire it to the bottom section.

## Structural Habits

Not word-level. These are the shapes my Chinese thinking imposes on English, and they cost more than vocabulary errors.

1. **One long message instead of three short ones.**
   Chinese tolerates a single dense paragraph. Google Chat does not. Split at every change of purpose: context / request / closing.

2. **Subject drift.**
   `you have posted a PR` when I posted it. Chinese drops subjects freely; English does not. Check every clause has the right subject.

3. **Reason before conclusion.**
   Chinese builds up to the point. EU colleagues expect the ask in sentence one, the reason after.

4. **Over-formality as politeness.**
   Translating 敬语 instinct into `I would like to kindly request that you...`. Natural politeness in workplace English is shorter, not longer: `Could you take a look when you get a chance?`

5. **Missing the soft closing.**
   Ending on the request feels abrupt in English. Add `No rush`, `Let me know if anything's unclear`, or `Thanks in advance`.
